Sphere-cover plan for medium-fruit crevice filling
==================================================

Goal
----
Cover the remaining near-surface crevice samples with a small number of
medium fruits. Large fruits stay fixed. Final fruit meshes may overlap existing
fruits or each other by at most 0.2 cm. The configured exclusion mesh remains a
hard obstacle; the inner-container mesh is only a target/density preference.

1. Fruit size catalogue
-----------------------
For each medium fruit mesh, center an approximate bounding sphere at the
center of its local bounding box. Record the maximum vertex distance from
that center as the outer radius. Also record an effective coverage radius:
the outer sphere is conservative for irregular or elongated fruit and should
not by itself determine whether a fruit fits. Scales come from a small
configurable discrete list (default 1.2, 1.1, 1.0, 0.9, 0.8). Each (fruit,
scale) pair is a pre-scaled variant: its own mesh, SDF, bounding box, outer
radius and effective radius, all computed once at load. The catalogue is the
list of variants.

2. Cover the target samples with spheres
----------------------------------------
Work from the currently uncovered crevice targets. Keep the existing surface,
inner-container, and exclusion target masks, but ensure broad openings have
samples; no covering method can fill a hole it never samples. Use spatially
uniform target weights so dense triangulation does not dominate the score.

Generate candidate sphere centers in free space around target clusters, not
at the target positions themselves. Useful seeds include target positions
offset along their free-space normals, local target centroids, and maxima of
a local free-space distance field. Try discrete radii derived from the medium
fruit catalogue and its allowed scales. A candidate covers a target when the
target lies inside its sphere with a small numerical margin.

Select candidates by weighted gain in *newly* covered targets, with a cost for
fruit count and excessive size. After choosing a sphere, update coverage and
locally relax neighboring sphere centers/radii; repeat until marginal gain is
small. A spatial hash or KD-tree should limit candidate scoring to nearby
targets rather than scanning the full target set for every candidate.

Require new spheres to grow from the boundary: each should be able to touch
an existing or newly accepted fruit, so the cover does not create floating
fruit in the middle of a large void. This contact may be with a fruit of any
size, not only a >14 cm support fruit.

3. Sphere collision is only a proposal filter
---------------------------------------------
Bounding spheres are deliberately conservative. Requiring proxy spheres to
remain within 0.2 cm of non-overlap would reject many valid mesh fits and
leave seams. Permit limited proxy overlap during search, but enforce the
0.2 cm bound on the actual fruit meshes. Reject centers that plainly violate
the container or exclusion mesh. Use exact mesh/SDF checks after swapping in
a fruit, including both candidate-to-existing and candidate-to-candidate
overlap. Keep contact with some existing or newly placed fruit as a final
acceptance condition.

4. Replace each sphere with a fruit
----------------------------------
Choose the fruit variant (kind and discrete scale) by matching its effective
radius to the selected sphere. Nudging adjusts position/orientation only; to
change size, swap in a neighboring scale variant and re-score. Align the scaled fruit's bounding-box center with the sphere
center. Try several orientations, then nudge position/orientation/scale within
a small trust region. Score the actual mesh for target coverage, contact,
container fit, exclusion fit, and overlap. If no fruit fits, leave those
targets uncovered and try another candidate rather than committing a proxy
that does not materialize as a valid fruit.

5. Scale via pre-scaled variants
--------------------------------
Do not make the geometry paths scale-aware. Instead, build each variant by
scaling the fruit mesh about its local origin (the same pivot Blender uses
for the scale field, so exported poses match) at load, then generate its SDF, samples and bounds from the
scaled mesh. Instances then use scale 1 in RigidTransform, so
RigidTransform::Apply, RightMultRigid, PackingScene::Put, SDF lookups, overlap
fractions, support-gap checks and NudgeToTarget need no changes.

Remaining work:
- Variant bookkeeping: variant id -> (base fruit, scale, mesh, SDF). Variants
  are created only for medium fruits; large fruits are untouched.
- Save/load: SaveInstances/LoadPack must round-trip the variant. Either store
  the base mesh name plus the scale field and have LoadPack resolve it to the
  variant (Blender's load_pack.py already applies scale), or save the variant
  mesh name. Prefer the first: the scale field already exists in the format.
  Export therefore writes the prototype fruit name with scale = variant
  scale; the translation is the variant's own pose, not rescaled.
- Check that no code path double-applies scale (variant mesh already scaled
  and the transform scale field also applied) when loading in the packer.

Verify by saving and reloading a pack with variants, then comparing bounds and
overlap results before/after reload, and checking Blender shows the same size.

6. Debug and validation
----------------------
Export proposed spheres as a Blender-visible mesh, chosen fruit instances as
a pack, and initially/finally uncovered targets as separate point objects.
Record per-candidate rejection reasons: container, exclusion, overlap,
missing contact, or insufficient coverage. First validate the strawberry2
opening around (-168.154, -60.4725, -48.5998), then test the full hand and
record runtime, fruit count, target coverage, and maximum measured overlap.

7. Wavefront cover, tucking and spawning (revised approach)
-----------------------------------------------------------
Seeding and growth. Seeds touch a fixed fruit; every later sphere is grown from
a wavefront so that it touches an existing sphere (gap about 0). A front
sphere with no uncovered targets within reach is retired. Targets are swept
min x, then y, then z. Proxy limits are tight: sphere-fruit and sphere-sphere
overlap at most 15% of the radius, container wall at most 30%.

Tucking (relaxation). After the wavefront, move the spheres together to remove
gaps, solving small spatial groups (about 20-50 spheres with a halo) rather
than all spheres at once. Neighboring spheres outside the group are fixed.
Per group, minimize over centers (optionally radii in a continuous range):
  - squared sphere-sphere overlap beyond the allowed slack;
  - squared overlap with fixed fruit, container and exclusion mesh;
  - a pull of near-touching pairs to exact contact, and covered targets kept
    covered.
Use projected gradient or L-BFGS, a few hundred iterations per group, and sweep
the groups Gauss-Seidel style until the energy stops dropping. The sphere-sphere
and sphere-container "phi functions" (Stoyan et al., Axioms 13(6) 396, 2024)
are just signed distances here: |ci-cj| - ri - rj and -containerSDF(c) - r.
The useful ideas taken from that work are the smooth overlap measure and the
decomposition into small subproblems.

How tucking treats existing fruit. Existing large fruit are static obstacles
and never move. Only spheres move.
  - Obstacle term: max(0, r - d(c) - slack)^2, where d is the signed distance
    to the nearest large fruit (the same SDF NearestFruit uses); its gradient is
    the SDF gradient, which pushes the sphere out along the fruit normal. The
    slack is the allowed fruit overlap (0.2 cm plus proxy error), so a sphere
    may rest against fruit without being pushed away.
  - Contact term: spheres within the contact tolerance of a fruit get a mild
    pull toward exact contact, (d - r)^2, so they stay tucked against it.
  - Support: a sphere stays valid only while it touches fruit or a chain of
    spheres that reaches fruit. Check after each sweep; drop or re-grow spheres
    that lose their chain.
  - Container wall and exclusion mesh are fixed obstacles with the same form
    (the exclusion mesh with a stricter slack). Medium fruit already in the pack
    are not obstacles: they are what this cover replaces (sphereSupportMinDiag).
  - Limit: the fruit SDF is coarse, so near-fruit results are accurate to about
    0.1 to 0.2 cm and the gradient may jitter at that scale.

Spawning. Tucking moves spheres, so targets may become uncovered again and new
gaps open. After each tuck sweep, regrow from the tucked spheres on whatever is
still uncovered, then tuck again. Alternate tuck and spawn until nothing
changes or an iteration cap is hit.

Targets (experiment). The old targets are hull triangle centroids from a 1 cm
marching-cubes closing (voxel stair error, uneven density, no size or depth
information). Replace them with a deterministic lattice of free-space points
inside the closing-minus-fruit volume at a finer grid:
  - fruit set: large fruit only (box diagonal above 14 cm), optionally only
    those with center x < sphereMaxX for local experiments;
  - crevice mask = closed(R) <= 0 and rawDistToFruit > minClearance, inside the
    container (approximately) and outside the exclusion mesh;
  - keep a regular lattice (sphereFieldSpacing) of mask voxel centers, so
    coverage is measured by volume, not by triangle count.
The inner 12 cm container mesh is ignored for this experiment.

8. Progress and learnings so far
--------------------------------
Implemented (SphereCover.cpp/.h, sphere* keys in PackingConfig, configs/pack_hand.cfg):
  - Radii: volume-equivalent radius of each medium kind times sphereScales
    (1.2 .. 0.8), merged within 0.25 cm: 12 radii, 1.78 to 4.80 cm.
  - Advancing-front cover (section 7): sweep seeds, grow touching spheres,
    retire exhausted front spheres. Proxy limits: sphere-fruit and
    sphere-sphere overlap 15% of radius, wall/exclusion 30%, contact within 0.8.
  - Debug region (sphereMaxX) and probe points (sphereProbe) that print nearby
    fruit, spheres, targets and a per-reason rejection tally for candidates.
  - Fast path for experiments: BuildSphereCoverScene loads only the meshes it
    needs plus the container SDF; RunSphereCoverFast adds only the large fruit
    with center x < sphereMaxX for distance queries (no background voxelization,
    no Put). 12 large fruit with x < -208: about 6 s end to end, down from 52 s.
  - Outputs: sphere_cover.txt (x y z r), sphere_cover_spheres.obj (merged ico
    spheres), sphere_cover_fruit.txt (the region's fruit, loadable with
    bpy_scripts/load_pack.py), sphere_cover_targets.obj,
    sphere_cover_uncovered.obj; bpy_scripts/load_spheres.py instances spheres.
  - Targets can be loaded from mediumTargetsFile (vertices of an obj) or
    regenerated at a fine voxel size (sphereFieldVoxel > 0).

Learnings:
  - Obstacles are only the fixed LARGE fruit (box diagonal > 14 cm). The pack
    already contained many medium fruit (orange, peach, plum, ...) around the
    probe points; treating them as obstacles made nearly every candidate fail
    the fruit-overlap check. Medium fruit are what this cover replaces.
  - Targets inside existing fruit are not targets. The loaded hull points had
    about 8,000 of 38,000 inside large fruit by the exact SDF.
  - Strict whole-sphere containment (75% -> 15% overlap) leaves gaps; loose
    overlap gives heavy overlap. Neither is right: the sphere proxy needs a
    better coverage definition, not just different thresholds.
  - Global greedy by gain always picks the largest sphere. A sweep plus a
    wavefront gives spheres that touch, which was the requirement.
  - Wall-ray targets (march from the container wall to the first fruit, keep
    columns deeper than their neighbors, count a target covered when a sphere
    blocks its line to the wall) were worse than the hull points and were
    removed. Most wall columns are deep everywhere, and a neighbor-median
    threshold mostly picked smooth bulk voids, not crevices.
  - Original hull targets are triangle centroids of a 1 cm marching-cubes
    closing: voxel stair error, uneven density, no size or depth information.
  - The shrinkwrap raw distance field is UNSIGNED: it is positive inside fruit.
    Using raw > 0 as "not inside fruit" puts fruit interiors in the crevice
    mask (11,000 of 13,450 lattice points were inside fruit). The mask must be
    restricted to voxels a flood from outside through free space reaches (which
    also drops sealed pockets). ComputeShrinkwrapField's raw > 0 test may have
    the same flaw; not checked.
  - A finer grid shrinks the real crevice volume: 12 fruit with x < -208 give
    1,986 free lattice points (1 cm, 0.4 cm voxels) after the flood fix.
  - Result on that region: 102 spheres cover 1,154 of 1,986 points (58%). The
    uncovered points are mostly within 1 to 2 cm of fruit, narrower than the
    smallest radius (1.78) under the overlap limits. Smaller radii (scales to
    about 0.5) or dropping sub-1 cm slivers are the open options.
  - Speed: do experiments on a small region. The full pipeline setup (all
    meshes, background grid, 1,104 Put calls) dominated the run time, not the
    cover itself (under 3 s).

9. Next idea: manufacturability from local convex hulls
-------------------------------------------------------
The manufacturing process has no difficulty making convex parts. So measure
how hard a spot is by how far it lies from the convex shape the surrounding
fruit would support locally, instead of from the 6/12 cm closing hull alone:
  - Take small groups of nearby large fruit (for example a fruit and its
    touching neighbors, or all fruit within a radius) and compute the convex
    hull of each group.
  - Regions far from a local hull surface are hard to make, so they are spots
    to plug. A plane pressed against a few fruit (a supporting plane of the
    group's hull) bridges the gap between them; the hard regions are the
    spaces between that plane and the fruit surfaces, and anything not
    reachable by such a plane.
  - Difficulty value per free-space point: distance from the point to the
    nearest local hull surface (or, equivalently, how deep the point lies in
    the pocket between a supporting plane and the fruit). Targets are the
    lattice points with difficulty above a threshold, weighted by it; spheres
    are sized and ordered by it.
  - Group choice matters: too large a group gives a hull that bridges real
    crevices; too small a group makes every dimple look hard. Sweep the group
    size (for example 2 to 6 fruit, or a radius of 6 to 12 cm) and keep points
    that stay hard across scales.
  - Tests on the x < -208 region first: compute hulls per group, color the
    lattice by difficulty, and compare to the closing-hull targets.

10. Crevice faces from a fruit neighbor graph (implemented, first pass)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Design (agreed):
  - Fruit set: the large and medium-large fruit from step 2 (about 1k on the
    full hand, all of pack_filtered_step2). For experiments, only those with
    center x < sphereMaxX (12 fruit for x < -208).
  - Neighbor graph: two fruit are neighbors when their SURFACE gap is at most
    hullGap (1.5 cm), not by center distance (eggplant/zucchini are long).
    Gap = min of one fruit's vertex samples against the other's SDF, both ways.
    The gap threshold is what prevents arches: no edge across a wide void.
  - Groups: the pack is 3D, so "every polygon of the graph" is not defined
    (no faces, exponentially many cycles). Use graph triangles (3-cliques) and
    chordless 4-cycles; skip a quad that has a chord (its triangles cover it).
    Longer cycles bring arches back.
  - Each crevice may need more than one fruit, often many, to fill.
  - A pressed plane that does not touch some fruit still leaves a crevice under
    it; that space still needs filling. Unbounded pockets are kept, capped.
  - Faces with any corner inside the inner container do not matter. Space near
    the exclusion mesh does not matter.
  - The goal is to fill as much crevice as possible with medium fruit.

Why it helps the plug check: a crevice becomes a concrete object, a mouth
polygon with an inward normal, a depth map and a width. To test whether a new
fruit or sphere plugged it, cast short rays inward from samples on the mouth
and sphere-trace them against the fruit SDFs plus the new object; the crevice
is plugged when the max ray depth falls below a tolerance (about 1 cm). Only
mouths near the new object need re-tracing. This is local, needs no voxel grid
or flood fill, and counts "under a sphere" automatically (a sphere in the mouth
stops the rays). It works the same for real fruit, given their SDF.

Implementation (CreviceFaces.cpp/.h, runs from the fast path when
sphereHullFaces is set; keys hullGap, hullSamples, hullMinDepth, hullExposeDist,
hullExclMargin):
  1. neighbor graph by surface gap (300 vertex samples per fruit);
  2. triangles and chordless quads, each found once;
  3. quickhull of each group's samples; keep faces whose corners come from at
     least two fruit (bridging faces);
  4. outside-facing: marching from the face center along its normal reaches the
     container wall before any fruit, within 30 cm; drop faces with a corner
     inside the inner container or within 2 cm of the exclusion mesh;
  5. pocket depth: 15 inward rays from the face to the first fruit; keep faces
     at least hullMinDepth deep (capped at 16 cm, capped faces kept);
  6. drop duplicates by quantized center and normal.
Outputs: crevice_faces.obj (fan-triangulated from v0), crevice_faces.txt
(depth, normal, group instance ids), fruit_graph.obj (one vertex per fruit at
its center of mass, one line per neighbor edge).

First result, x < -208 (12 fruit), 0.8 s: 23 edges, 13 triangles, 4 chordless
quads; 1,412 bridging faces, dropped 266 inner, 251 not facing outside, 373
duplicates, 0 shallow; 522 kept. Depths mostly 4 to 16 cm, 41 at the cap.
Total face area about 12,500 cm2, far too much for 12 fruit: neighboring groups
share fruit and produce overlapping faces over the same pocket with different
centers, which the duplicate key does not catch.

Next:
  - Overlap reduction: where faces from different groups overlap over the same
    pocket, keep only the outermost one (the plate a manufactured surface would
    follow).
  - Merge adjacent near-coplanar faces into one polygon per mouth.
  - Check the graph and faces in Blender against the visible crevices before
    using the mouths as the cover's targets and plug test.

11. Local hull-face revision (x < -208 experiment)
-------------------------------------------------
The first-pass whole-face test kept large overlapping internal sheets while
missing visible gaps. The current CreviceFaces implementation uses:
  - Surface-gap graph edges up to 1.5 cm for triangles and chordless quads,
    plus up to four sparse pair neighbors per fruit across gaps up to 8 cm.
    The wider pairs catch two-fruit grooves without creating high-order hulls
    across large arbitrary regions.
  - Convex-hull triangles split into approximately 2 cm patches. Each patch
    must be near the outer container surface, outside the inner/exclusion
    masks and all fruit, open in its outward normal direction, and over at
    least one free crevice lattice point.
  - A lattice point is assigned to a patch only when its projection along the
    patch normal falls within the triangle (with a small seam tolerance) and
    is within 16 cm. The second-nearest fruit in the hull group must be within
    12 cm OF THE TARGET, not the patch. A broad hull mouth may be far from its
    second fruit while the pocket beneath lies tightly between two fruit.
  - Inward ray depth is a quality cutoff for the patch; it is not used to
    truncate target assignment, because the centroid ray can strike a fruit
    shoulder beside a deeper channel. Select patches greedily by the number
    of new lattice points they represent. This removes redundant faces from
    overlapping group hulls.

The largest previously missed cluster was between watermelon3 and watermelon2
around (-222.8, -48.9, -52.3). All 53 examined targets lay inside the pair
hull and projected beneath mixed-owner hull faces. The patch-centroid support
test rejected their mouths because one fruit was over 12 cm from the patch;
checking support at the target restored this gap. Relaxing the container band
from 12 to 100 cm recovered only one target, so it was not the main issue.

The final x < -208 scene selects 13 fruit by world-space bounding-box center.
It yielded 932 selected patches representing 1953/1980 free lattice targets
(98.6%); the remaining 27 are small scattered clusters. Face generation took
3.5 s and the full fast-path run about 8.1 s. These counts measure which
sampled pockets have candidate mouth patches, NOT actual medium-fruit
coverage. The sphere cover algorithm is unchanged and still covered 1093/1980
samples with 106 spheres. The earlier position-based 12-fruit selection had
1965/1986 candidate-covered targets; its count is not directly comparable
because the fruit subset and target lattice changed.

Debug outputs: crevice_faces.obj (selected patches),
crevice_faces_raw_bridges.obj (unfiltered mixed-owner hull triangles),
crevice_faces_claimed_targets.obj and crevice_faces_unclaimed_targets.obj,
fruit_graph.obj, and crevice_faces.txt (depth, container distance, target count,
normal and group IDs). The Blender inspection script is
bpy_scripts/inspect_crevice_faces.py. It imports OBJ with Y-forward/Z-up,
which is required to preserve this pack's coordinates.

12. Replace hull patches with surface-derived passage centers
---------------------------------------------------------------
The hull triangles are artificial bridging planes, not crevice surfaces. A
target up to 16 cm behind a triangle could justify a patch far from the
visible opening. They are useful as a diagnostic only and are disabled in the
new local experiment (sphereHullFaces 0, sphereMouths 1).

The outer hand mesh is the container; anything inside
hand4.8m_inner12cm.obj is outside our objective. GenerateFieldTargets now
rejects points inside the inner mesh. On x < -208 this removes 317 lattice
points before sphere fitting.

CreviceMouths.cpp instead starts with the remaining exposed, fruit-free
crevice samples. For each sample it finds the nearest two fruit SDFs and their
surface normals. It keeps pairs whose normals face each other (dot < -0.2),
projects toward each surface, and uses the midpoint of those surface points
as a passage center. The midpoint must be free of fruit and exclusion mesh,
outside the inner mesh, and within 12 cm of the outer hand surface. Repeated
centers are collapsed by fruit pair and 1.5 cm cell; short same-pair links
form a graph of local grooves. This produces points in the visible seams,
without long or remote triangles.

The center-to-center surface distance estimates passage width. Centers at
least 2*(smallest medium sphere radius - 0.2 cm) wide are potentially useful
for medium spheres; this is only a width screen, not a full collision test.
The sphere cover can multiply nearby volume-target weights by
mouthTargetWeight and seed those targets first. Its normal collision and
container checks still decide whether a sphere can be placed.

Local test, 13 fruit whose world-space bounding-box center has x < -208:
  - 1,690 fruit-free outer-shell targets, 558 distinct passage centers, 204
    with estimated width >= 3.166 cm for the smallest medium sphere.
  - Baseline with mouth guidance off: 108 spheres, 985/1690 volume targets
    and 121/204 medium-width passage centers covered.
  - mouthTargetWeight 2: 114 spheres, 1026/1690 targets, 144/204 centers.
  - mouthTargetWeight 4: 117 spheres, 1022/1690 targets, 151/204 centers.
  - mouthTargetWeight 8: 114 spheres, 1003/1690 targets, 148/204 centers.
Weight 4 is the current local setting because it covers the most passage
centers while retaining more overall target coverage than the baseline.
The full run takes about 6 s on this subset. The remaining 53 medium-width
centers form 32 spatial clusters at a 2.5 cm link distance (largest cluster
has four centers). They are saved separately for visual inspection; width
alone does not prove they are fillable without a more detailed collision
search.

Outputs in out_hand_mouth_x208: crevice_mouth_points.obj (all centers),
crevice_mouth_medium_width.obj, crevice_mouth_medium_covered.obj,
crevice_mouth_medium_uncovered.obj, crevice_mouth_graph.obj,
crevice_mouth_points.txt (width, outer distance, fruit pair, source target),
and sphere_cover.txt. bpy_scripts/inspect_crevice_mouths.py makes a Blender
scene with separate collections for fruit, all centers, covered/uncovered
medium-width centers, and wireframe spheres.

13. Direct mouth coverage and tight sphere contacts
---------------------------------------------------
The previous mouth weight only marked the nearest lattice target, and 69 of
the 387 medium-width centers in the wider field had no target within 2 cm.
It did not reward a sphere for plugging those centers. Coverer now adds a
direct gain for each uncovered mouth center inside a candidate sphere, keeps
the covered state incrementally, and runs a global mouth-first greedy phase
before the older local front-growth phase. Candidates are evaluated across
all open centers and medium radii; local refinement improves the best one.

Sphere-to-fruit and sphere-to-sphere overlap is limited to 0.2 cm absolute,
and a new sphere must contact a fruit or an existing sphere within 0.15 cm.
The coarse fruit SDF can err by several millimeters, so each accepted sphere
is also checked against the actual fruit triangles. On the 13-fruit x < -208
subset, the resulting spheres have nearest sphere gap >= -0.199 cm and
nearest fruit mesh-vertex gap >= -0.194 cm; nearest contact gap <= 0.036 cm.

Use radius 12 cm with 1 cm field voxels. A 12 cm radius with 0.4 cm voxels
exceeds AdapDF's 16-voxel band and produces an invalid closing field. The
12/1 field gives 4063 fruit-free outer-shell samples and 387 nominally
medium-width mouth centers. With mouthGainWeight 6 and 14 candidate positions,
109 spheres cover 3041/4063 samples and 336/387 centers in about 13 s total.
For comparison, the stricter 0.2 cm overlap version without a mouth-first
phase covered 2576/4063 samples and 251/387 centers. The remaining 51 open
centers include cases that pass the two-fruit width estimate but may fail the
full fruit, container, and already-placed-sphere checks. More candidate
offsets alone are unlikely to resolve all of them.

The tested configuration is configs/pack_hand_mouth_x208.cfg, and the debug
output is out_hand_mouth_r12v1_gain14w6_x208. Blender scene and renders are
under debug_crevice_faces_x208/gain14w6_r12. pack_hand.cfg uses the same
packing parameters; its exclusion and inner-container meshes are retained.

14. Reject low-gain front placements
------------------------------------
The front-growth and seed paths previously accepted any collision-valid
candidate, even when Gain - Cost <= 0. Sphere #77 in the previous local run,
at (-222.627, -49.788, -31.517), had radius 1.783 cm and covered only one
new lattice target and zero medium-width mouths. Its unweighted gain was 1,
versus cost 1.178, so the placement was counterproductive. It did contact two
earlier spheres (gaps -0.082 and -0.050 cm) and a fruit (nearest sampled
vertex gap 0.013 cm), but contact alone did not make it useful. Seeds and
front growth now require strictly positive score. The corrected local run
has 103 spheres instead of 109, covers 3045/4063 targets instead of 3041,
and still covers 336/387 medium-width mouths. Nine candidate placement
attempts were rejected for nonpositive gain. The corrected debug output is
out_hand_mouth_r12v1_positive_x208 and the Blender scene is under
debug_crevice_faces_x208/positive_r12.

15. Audit the objective mismatch in the front phase
---------------------------------------------------
The spheres at (-245.299, -53.035, -51.977) and
(-250.016, -42.814, -56.575) were both radius 1.783 cm. Each gained two
lattice targets and zero medium-width crevice centers. Their nearest
detected mouths were about 2.99 and 3.94 cm away. Both touched an earlier
sphere at -0.05 cm gap, so collision/contact was not the failure. Their
volume gain of 2 exceeded cost 1.178, allowing the front phase to keep them.

An audit of every sphere in the positive-score run shows the systematic
problem: the 55 mouth-first spheres gained 333 medium-width mouth centers;
the next 48 front-growth spheres gained only three more, while 45 gained
none. Those 45 gained 377 shrinkwrap lattice samples, so the front phase
was optimizing a different objective. The 12 cm closing field contains
1337/4063 samples farther than 2 cm from any detected mouth and 198 farther
than 4 cm. These samples may represent open volume, but they are not proof
of a useful medium-fruit crevice placement.

The new optional sphereFrontRequireMouth setting requires each front-phase
sphere to cover at least one previously uncovered medium-width mouth. On the
same x < -208 subset it produces 59 spheres total, covering 337/387 mouths
and 2694/4063 lattice samples. The two cited spheres are absent, and all
four front-phase spheres gain a mouth. This prioritizes visible crevices
over broad shrinkwrap volume. The tested output is
out_hand_mouth_r12v1_mouthfront_x208 and the Blender scene is under
debug_crevice_faces_x208/mouthfront_r12.

16. Feasibility before and after packing; local repair
------------------------------------------------------
A 1 cm center grid over all radii found a legal, positive-score medium
sphere covering 385/387 mouth centers before packing. After the 59-sphere
mouth-focused pack, it found none for the 50 open centers. Candidate
rejection counts for those 50 were 107003 fruit-SDF clearance, 6711
container, and 2086 existing-sphere overlap, with zero legal positions.
Thus the two-fruit width heuristic does not certify fit, but the main
failure is greedy arrangement: most centers are individually fillable yet
become blocked by earlier spheres that do not cover them. CSV diagnostics
are sphere_open_mouth_feasibility_before.csv and _after.csv.

A bounded local repair now searches a 1 cm grid around each open center,
allows a candidate to replace at most two conflicting spheres, and accepts
a swap only if the total number of covered mouth centers increases. It
also checks that removing support spheres does not leave retained spheres
without a fruit or sphere contact. On the local region, two one-for-one
swaps raised coverage from 337/387 to 340/387 without increasing the 59
spheres. The remaining 47 centers have no legal candidate on the same grid
after repair. Overall lattice coverage is 2642/4063; this target count is
intentionally lower than the volume-oriented cover because the objective
now prioritizes visible crevices. The current Blender scene is under
debug_crevice_faces_x208/mouthfront_repair_r12.
